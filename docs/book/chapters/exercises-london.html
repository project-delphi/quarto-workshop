Exercises for Part II

Keywords

Quarto, reproducible publishing, coding assistants, Markdown, Python, EPUB, data analysis, k-means clustering

The first five exercises collect Part II’s hands-on work in order. Each names the chapter that explains it, how you know you are done, and the line that catches you up if you are not; a checkpoint is the state at the end of its block, so after catching up, skip to the next exercise. Appendix E — Checkpoints at a Glance, and Answers has the whole table. The rest leave the prompts out. In the transfer tasks you write the prompt and the check yourself, and the review questions test whether the reasons stuck; short answers are at the end.

Start the assistant in ~/london-guide/, next to ~/quarto-workshop/. Catching up reads outside that folder, so approve the assistant’s request to read ../quarto-workshop.

Exercise II.1: London in Markdown

From 4  Markdown, Rendering and Project Rules. Write london.md with a heading, lists, a link, the Big Ben photo with alt text and credit, a quotation, a table from data/sights.csv and a code fence; render it and keep a live preview; write the project’s AGENTS.md; give the folder its own environment with uv.

You're done when london.html shows both lists, the photo with its credit, the five-row table and the checklist, and uv run quarto check jupyter reports the Python in this folder’s .venv.

Stuck for two minutes? Paste this into your assistant, then rejoin at the next exercise:

Catch me up: follow ../quarto-workshop/checkpoints/01-london-markdown/README.md

Exercise II.2: a document that computes

From 5  Documents That Compute. Rename the guide to london.qmd with front matter, add a cell that reads data/attractions.csv and a sentence with inline code, add a captioned, labelled bar chart, then render to HTML, Word and a Typst PDF (and, optionally, PowerPoint, EPUB and Reveal.js).

You're done when london.html shows the title block, the computed sentence and a numbered chart, and london.docx and london.pdf show the same sentence and chart.

Stuck for two minutes? Paste this into your assistant, then rejoin at the next exercise:

Catch me up: follow ../quarto-workshop/checkpoints/02-london-qmd/README.md

Exercise II.3: three audiences at once

From 6  One Guide, Five Audiences. With one prompt and three sub-agents, write the dashboard, the Word brief with its covering email, and the A5 flyer; the lead renders them one at a time.

You're done when london-dashboard.html has an Attractions page with three value boxes, a bar chart and a line chart, and a Churches page listing ten churches with Visit London links; london-email.docx opens with headings and working links; london-email-message.md holds the email in one text block; london-flyer.pdf is one A5 page.

Stuck for two minutes? Paste this into your assistant, then rejoin at the next exercise:

Catch me up: follow ../quarto-workshop/checkpoints/04-london-audiences/README.md

There is no checkpoint 03: the workshop’s block numbering skips it.

Exercise II.4: a published website

From 7  Publishing and Proving the Rebuild. Make the folder a website with a navigation bar and a Downloads menu, put it on GitHub, and publish it with an interactive uv run quarto publish gh-pages.

You're done when https://<your-github-username>.github.io/london-guide/london-dashboard.html opens on your phone.

Stuck for two minutes? Paste this into your assistant, then rejoin at the next exercise:

Catch me up: follow ../quarto-workshop/examples/london/README.md

If you have already published, the catch-up copies the finished project’s README over yours; restore your own with git checkout -- README.md.

Exercise II.5: change an input, explain the rebuild

From Change one input, explain the rebuild, with no prompt supplied. Set the Tower of London’s visits_2025 to 8000000, predict which outputs change, rebuild, explain what changed and what did not, and restore the real value with git restore data/attractions.csv.

You're done when you can name the outputs that changed, the ones that did not, and the line of code that links each changed output to the input. Model answers are in Appendix E — Checkpoints at a Glance, and Answers.

Write your own prompts

These build on Part II and have no checkpoints. For each, write the prompt yourself, as concrete as the book’s (the files, the columns, the output) and ending with a check you can verify without trusting the assistant’s report. If you skipped them, the optional pieces come first: the square advert (A square advert (optional)), the white paper (A white paper (optional)) and the three.js page (The sights in three dimensions (optional)).

  1. A handout of free attractions. The office wants a one-page A5 handout of the attractions in data/attractions.csv that are free to enter, each with its 2025 visits. Decide which admission values count as free and say so in the prompt. You are done when the handout lists as many attractions as the dashboard’s “Top 10 with free entry” value box counts, and no number on it was typed.
  2. Then and now. Add a section to london.qmd comparing each of the five sights’ 2025 visits with 2019’s, from data/visits.csv. Your prompt should say why 2019 is the year to compare with, and ask for the one sight whose counts are not like-for-like to be flagged. You are done when every percentage comes from a cell and the flagged sight is the one data/README.md names.
  3. Turn a copy into a computation. The flyer and the sights tables in the guide and the partner brief all copy their facts from data/sights.csv (Table 7.1). Make the flyer’s table read the file instead. Your check: change one station name in sights.csv yourself, rebuild, and see it on the flyer. Restore the file afterwards.
  4. A map in two dimensions. The 3-D map’s data are in data/places.csv and the borough boundaries in data/london-boroughs.geojson. Add a third dashboard page that draws the sights and churches on a 2-D Plotly map from those files, with each point’s source in its hover text. Decide how you will check that every point is where it should be.
  5. Update the live site. Change the guide’s welcome sentence, then write one prompt that commits with a message that says why and publishes the update with uv run quarto publish gh-pages --no-prompt. You are done when the new sentence is on your phone.

Review questions

  1. The project’s rules say “ALVA member attractions”, never “London’s most visited attractions”. What would be wrong with the second phrase?
  2. While quarto preview runs on london.qmd, the assistant runs quarto render london.qmd and it fails with readfile 'london.quarto_ipynb'. What happened, and what are two ways to avoid it?
  3. The guide’s bar chart has a caption and alt text. What should each say, and why are they not the same?
  4. You need two changes to london-dashboard.qmd: a new value box and a fix to the line chart’s legend. Should you give them to two sub-agents working in parallel?
  5. After the rebuild in Exercise II.5, the dashboard on your computer shows the Tower of London as the most visited attraction, but the flyer has not changed and the dashboard on your phone still shows the old numbers. Explain both.

Answers

  1. ALVA counts only its member sites, so its table is not a list of every London attraction. “London’s most visited attractions” would claim more than the data show (Rules for the guide).
  2. A render writes a temporary notebook named after the file, and the preview was rendering the same file at the same moment, so the two collided. Let the preview do the rendering while it runs, or stop it with Ctrl+C first and restart it afterwards (What a render does, and why two renders collide).
  3. The caption says what the chart is: the measure, the year and whose attractions. The alt text says what it shows, such as which bar is longest and which shortest, for a reader who cannot see it (A chart with a caption and alt text).
  4. No. Both edit the same file, and both are small. Sub-agents suit independent, substantial tasks that write different files; make these two edits in turn (Sub-agents: one lead, many hands).
  5. The flyer loads no data at all: its facts were copied into its Markdown, so a change to attractions.csv cannot reach it. The live site shows what was last published, and changes only when you publish again (Change one input, explain the rebuild).
